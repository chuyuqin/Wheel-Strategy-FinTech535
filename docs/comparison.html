Does stock choice change the wheel?

AMZN · Coca-Cola · Procter & Gamble · Verizon

The same cash-secured wheel is tested on four stocks. Each gets a $50,000 account and is compared with owning that stock, including dividends. The shortlist and rules stay fixed; every result is reported.

$50,000 account · Cash-secured · No borrowing · Historical LSEG quotes

The strategy in three rules

1 / START WITH CASH

Sell a 45-delta put

Choose a strike below the stock price, about 21 days from expiration. This earns premium and may result in buying shares at the strike.

2 / IF ASSIGNED SHARES

Sell a 15-delta call

Choose a strike above the stock price and at least the shares’ purchase price. The lower call delta leaves more room for the stock to rise.

3 / CONTROL THE COMMITMENT

Use up to 90% of cash

Size puts in whole 100-share contracts. Keep the rest as a buffer. Calls cover owned shares. Hold to expiration, then repeat.

Delta helps choose how close the strike is to the stock price; it is not an exact assignment probability. A purchase-price floor on calls does not protect the shares from falling.

Why these choices, and how were they selected?

The shortlist consists of AMZN and three mature-business candidates named before this comparison. It is not a random sample of stocks. A 90% allocation budget puts more of the account to work than the first one-contract design while retaining a cash buffer. Whole contracts can leave more than 10% unused. The call strike floor avoids agreeing to sell shares below their gross purchase price, at the cost of sometimes collecting no call premium.

The original AMZN study compared eight settings combining put delta 0.30 or 0.45, call delta 0.15 or 0.30, and approximately three- or five-week maturities. The setting with the highest net 2023 AMZN return was selected. It was then applied unchanged to 2024, the already examined 2025 sample, and a separate 2026 sample downloaded after the rules were frozen.

Execution assumptions, data audit and reproduction
  1. Prior-session decisions. Rank eligible monthly options for the selected stock by distance to target delta, then maturity, strike and RIC. Three-week settings allow 14–28 calendar DTE; five-week settings allow 28–45. Delta must be within 0.10 of its target. Puts are below the stock close; calls are above it and at least the assigned purchase price.
  2. Observed fills. Require signal-day volume ≥ 100, open interest ≥ 500, a positive two-sided quote, and spread ≤ $1 and ≤ 25% of midpoint. Fill the selected option at the next session’s bid only if its quote passes the same price filters; otherwise skip. No execution-day contract substitution. Buybacks use ask, stock sales use bid, and option commission is $0.65 per contract per side. Stock and assignment fees are $0.
  3. Cash and assignment. Put lots = floor(90% × available cash ÷ (100 × strike)), before receiving premium. Calls cover the owned shares. At expiration, options at least $0.01 in the money are assumed assigned. The option is removed and 100 shares per contract transfer at the strike. All lots in a batch use the same assignment convention. There are no discretionary rolls; the final date closes remaining positions.
  4. Accounting limits. The ledger books economic obligations on event dates; it does not reproduce legal settlement balances. Stock marks use bid; option liabilities use ask. Missing asks can be carried for at most two sessions for valuation, never execution. A longer gap or missing terminal price fails the run. Taxes, regulatory fees, intraday quote synchronization and actual exercise notices are omitted.

For the original AMZN continuing 2023–2025 account, the median opening spread was $0.05 per share and the 90th percentile was $0.13. Selling at bid rather than midpoint reduced receipts by $450.50 across 126 contracts. Observed activity and spreads justify charging a measurable execution cost; daily quotes do not guarantee fill freshness or depth.

LSEG supplies 149,106 normalized option-day observations for the earlier sample and 43,426 for the 2026 sample. All 102 response checksums passed. Independent replays reconciled 856 events and 8,147 daily portfolios across all selected, parameter, and stress runs. 21 engine tests passed. The comparison uses fixed monthly strike grids: AMZN $40–$300 by $5; KO $20–$120 by $2.50; PG $60–$250 by $5; VZ $10–$80 by $2.50. Different stock price levels need different standard strike spacing. Missing series are not assumed listed. All four selected stocks survive in this sample; survivorship and shortlist-selection bias remain. Historical returns do not establish future profitability.

Dividend accounting and comparison audit

Ownership before ex-date trading earns a gross dividend receivable. Cash is credited on the payment date, or the next observed session. The benchmark reinvests paid cash at the stock ask; dividends are not counted twice or received merely because shares were purchased on the ex-date. Unpaid claims remain in final equity after the stock is sold.

The separate pre-dividend scenario assigns an in-the-money covered call at the close before ex-date if its bid-side time value is no greater than the announced dividend. The announcement must already be known. The writer then loses the upcoming dividend. This illustrates exercise sensitivity; actual holder instructions are unavailable. The scenario can improve or worsen returns because it changes the timing of share delivery and subsequent put sales; it is not a worst-case bound.
