The belief

Investment thesis

One-sentence thesis: Outside earnings periods and strong price trends, demand for downside protection may price MSFT puts above their expected losses, creating an opportunity for cash-secured put selling after trading costs and the opportunity cost of cash.

Economic rationale

Investors buy puts to protect against a decline in MSFT. The put seller receives a premium in exchange for the obligation to buy shares at the strike if assigned. The thesis is that protection demand can sometimes make this premium greater than the expected loss and costs of accepting that obligation.

Higher premiums can also reflect higher risk. During a selloff, the additional premium may be insufficient to cover larger losses. Low recent volatility is therefore an entry screen, rather than evidence by itself that an option is overpriced.

Entry criteria

A new position requires both suitable market conditions and an attractive executable premium. The tracker below evaluates recent price behavior. Option quotes are then needed to assess whether the premium compensates for downside exposure and the capital reserved.

Following put assignment, the account may sell a covered call against the shares. This earns another premium but caps gains above the call strike. Each call therefore needs a separate assessment of the premium received and the upside surrendered.

Horizon & implementation

Holding period and trading rules

Thesis horizon

The initial thesis horizon is 3–6 months, with a monthly review. The opportunity may persist while demand for protection supports premiums above expected losses and costs. It can disappear as option prices or the underlying risk change.

Expected holding period

The expected holding period is 21–35 calendar days per option, consistent with the current minimum of 21 days to expiry. The actual average will be measured from completed trades. Shares acquired through assignment may remain in the account for several option cycles.

Current rules and planned changes

Current implementation: $50,000 initial paper capital and at most one contract at a time. Put strikes start at least 5% below the reference stock price and move lower if needed to keep the purchase obligation within the 90% cash limit. Call strikes are at least 5% above the reference price and no lower than the shares’ purchase basis. The minimum requested premium per share is 0.5% of the reference stock price.

Proposed extension: Apply the market-condition filter below, avoid expiries that span an earnings announcement, and review executable option quotes before entry. These checks have not yet been incorporated into the nightly order script.

Option pricing and performance

The pricing test will compare executable option bids with estimated losses, trading costs and the return available on cash. Performance will be measured using total account value, including unrealized gains and losses on shares and open options, against Treasury cash and MSFT total return.

The current dataset contains stock prices but no option quotes. The charts identify market conditions that may be unsuitable for entry; establishing an option-pricing advantage requires quote and trade data.

Three wrong-if conditions

When to stop or reassess

Volatility exceeds 30%Pause new trades if volatility, calculated from the latest 21 daily returns and put on a yearly scale, exceeds 30%. Check whether premiums are high enough for the larger price swings.
The price moves outside the range limitsPause if MSFT rises or falls more than 5% over 21 trading days, or falls more than 10% below its highest close in the latest 21 days. A sharp rise can make covered calls miss out on gains. A sharp fall can leave the account buying shares worth less than the agreed price.
Persistent underperformance versus cashOnce fills and daily account values are available, compare cumulative net return with a Treasury-cash benchmark using the same starting capital and date. Pause new entries and reassess the thesis if the strategy remains below that benchmark for 20 consecutive trading sessions.

These limits were chosen after seeing the available data and have not yet been tested on future trades. The charts calculate the first two rules. The third needs actual trades, daily option values and cash interest rates. Resume new trades after five trading days within the price limits and another check of earnings dates and option quotes. A pause does not sell existing shares or close existing options.

Rough alpha & costs

Premiums, losses and the cash alternative

Estimated net advantage ≈ premium received − expected option loss − trading costs − cash opportunity cost

Consider one $445 put sold for $2.62 per share. The account receives $262 and reserves $44,500 against assignment. After a $0.65 opening fee and $10 of assumed slippage—a less favorable execution price—$251.35 remains before option losses, or about 0.50% of the $50,000 account. Macro environment

How macro conditions affect the strategy

Rates and the value of waiting

When interest rates are higher, holding cash becomes more attractive. Selling options must earn enough to justify the extra risk. The comparison should use the interest actually paid on available cash and on money reserved for option obligations.

A tech selloff

When tech stocks fall, investors may pay more for protection. But MSFT may also fall further. A put seller can be required to buy shares above their market price, so higher premiums need to be weighed against larger possible losses.

A strong AI-led rally

If MSFT rises quickly, owning the shares may earn more than the wheel. A put seller may stay in cash and miss the rise. A covered-call seller may have to sell shares at the strike even when the market price is much higher.

Company-specific events

Microsoft earnings, cloud demand, AI spending and profit margins can cause sudden price changes. Recent price data may not capture that risk. The planned strategy avoids holding options across earnings announcements and checks company news before opening a trade.

Methodology & evidence

Data and evaluation

Available data

The sample contains 41 daily MSFT closes. After the first 21 days, there is enough history to calculate 20 sets of readings. This is a short sample, so it cannot show whether the rules will lead to profitable trades. The data comes from the course server.

Forward evaluation

Compare filtered and unfiltered MSFT wheel accounts using identical starting capital, order rules, costs and interest assumptions. Evaluate net return, maximum drawdown—the largest decline from an account high—and risk-adjusted performance. MSFT total return and Treasury cash provide additional benchmarks. Hold the rules fixed while collecting new observations.

Earlier wheel study

The earlier PG wheel study returned 24.78%, compared with 19.27% from buying and holding PG, from January 2023 through September 2024. PG was chosen after comparing several stocks, and that study used different option rules. Its results are background research; they do not show that the MSFT strategy or the new pause rules will work.
