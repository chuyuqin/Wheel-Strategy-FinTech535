AMZN · KO · PG · VZ — matched-window performance, Treasury-adjusted Sharpe, cash interest and assignment analysis.

I focused on PG after seeing the results for all four stocks. I now show the full comparison together. These are still the same historical observations, so they do not provide a fresh test of that choice.

A testable thesis and its mechanism

Prediction: for the fixed AMZN, KO, PG and VZ universe, annual wheel-minus-buy-and-hold total returns will be negatively rank-correlated with the stocks’ annual dividend-reinvested buy-and-hold returns. The lower two stocks by buy-and-hold return will have a higher median wheel excess return than the upper two. Evaluate both inequalities on the full 2027 calendar year with the present rules, all four names and identical dates; do not replace a loser or tune parameters from those outcomes.

Mechanism: puts can leave the account in cash during a rally; covered calls sell upside beyond their strikes. Premiums and cash interest can offset these opportunity costs when gains are modest, but do not eliminate losses after assignment. The prediction concerns relative performance across stock paths, not a promise that the wheel earns a positive absolute return or beats cash.

I wrote this prediction on November 16, 2026, after seeing the historical results. I would test it on the 2027 data with the rules fixed in advance. The old results help explain the idea, but they are not an independent test of it.

AMZN: buy-and-hold gained 117.01%, versus 66.30% for the wheel. The wheel owned shares on only 28.77% of sessions and had average stock value equal to 20.04% of equity. Its very large underlying rally makes this limited participation and called-away upside costly. Premium receipts alone are not a fair comparison with the uncapped underlying gain.

PG: buy-and-hold gained 19.27%, versus 24.78% for the wheel. A more modest total gain makes the premium cushion more competitive with foregone upside. But modest gains alone cannot explain PG’s win, because KO and VZ also had modest total gains. The distinguishing observed wheel path had 4 put assignments and 3 call assignments, producing $3,951.00 of net stock cash-flow gains plus $6,216.00 in premiums net of buybacks. KO and VZ each had only one put assignment and one call assignment, with zero net stock cash-flow gain over the complete liquidated sample. PG’s outcome therefore combined recurring option income with favorable assignment-to-delivery stock gains, rather than premium collection alone.

KO and VZ: buy-and-hold gained 20.39% and 26.04%, respectively. Their wheel results were 11.66% and 21.83%. Dividend timing also matters: KO’s wheel earned $1,627.50 of dividends versus $2,679.70 in buy-and-hold; VZ’s earned $3,630.00 versus $6,061.72. The wheel earns dividends only when it owns shares before ex-date. A high dividend yield alone therefore does not imply a wheel advantage.

Cost-basis floor: requiring a call strike at least equal to the shares’ gross purchase price can leave the account long shares without an eligible call after a decline. This retains downside while sometimes preventing premium collection. It avoids committing to sell below gross purchase price; it does not prevent an economic loss. “No eligible call” counts also include delta, maturity and liquidity filters, so the count is not attributed entirely to the floor. The matched no-floor diagnostic below improves KO and PG but reduces VZ’s return; removing the floor is not a universal repair.

Historical rates, cash yield and Sharpe

  • Source: Federal Reserve H.15 three-month constant-maturity Treasury yield via FRED (DGS3MO). The downloaded daily observations, source URL, retrieval time and SHA-256 are retained. This is an investment-yield series, not a Treasury total-return index.
  • No same-day rate lookahead: accrue each calendar day using only the latest nonmissing observation strictly earlier than that day. Weekends and market holidays earn calendar-day interest. Missing observations are carried forward only from the past, with a ten-day staleness limit. Historical observations were downloaded now and can reflect revisions; this is not an ALFRED vintage reconstruction.
  • Eligible cash: previous-close positive cash minus the full short-put strike obligation; a dividend receivable is excluded until payment. Reserved put collateral earns no interest in this conservative main convention. Multiply eligible cash by the calendar-day compounded ACT/365 factor. The same residual-cash convention applies to both buy-and-hold benchmarks. This cash yield is a modeled opportunity-cost proxy, not a claim that a broker pays the full Treasury rate.
  • Economic rerun: interest is posted to spendable cash before new trades. Position sizes are recomputed from the changed cash balance; this is not an ex-post return boost added to the old equity curve. The option-selection parameters, fee assumptions, stock list and historical dates are held fixed.
  • Risk-free excess Sharpe: for each observed session interval, account return minus the Treasury cash-accrual return for that same interval. Sharpe = mean(excess) / sample standard deviation(excess) × √252. The first observation includes execution friction with a zero risk-free interval because inception is the first session close. Volatility and drawdown continue to use total account returns. Annualizing daily Sharpe assumes conventional trading-day scaling and does not adjust for serial correlation.
  • Costs and comparison: $0.65 option fee per contract per side, bid-side short sales and ask-side buybacks, dividends and assignment follow the archived study. All-stock price histories and option coverage still end at the same pre-existing common date. No raw licensed LSEG data are published here.

Limitations

The historical window ends in September 2024 because that was the last common complete cached quarter after the data-download quota constrained the previous project. This remains a limitation rather than an economic justification for the period. Newer complete stock, option and dividend data are needed to evaluate a genuinely later period. Daily quote proxies do not establish actual fills, queue priority, intraday NBBO or real early exercise. Survivorship, the small selected universe, prior parameter selection and this retrospective explanation remain limitations.

All revised wheel and benchmark runs passed independent event-to-cash replay, equity identities, interest-principal eligibility, calendar-day accrual and excess-return checks.
