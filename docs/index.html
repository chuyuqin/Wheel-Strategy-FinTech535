PG Wheel Strategy

Cash-secured puts. Assigned shares. Covered calls.

A historical case study of option income on Procter & Gamble. Starting with $50,000, the wheel finished at $61,252.94, compared with $59,634.45 for dividend-reinvesting buy-and-hold.

January 3, 2023 – September 30, 2024 · No borrowing · Historical LSEG observations

Download submission ZIPView results ↓

Three trading rules

1 / CASH-SECURED PUT

Sell a 45-delta put

Choose a strike below PG’s stock price, targeting 21 calendar days to expiry. Reserve enough cash to buy every share if assigned.

2 / COVERED CALL

Sell a 15-delta call

After assignment, sell calls against the owned shares. Require a strike above the stock price and at least its gross purchase price.

3 / REPEAT THE CYCLE

Keep the account funded

Put obligations use up to 90% of available cash, in whole 100-share contracts. Hold to expiry; after call delivery, return to puts.

Research question

Can option premiums improve the return from owning PG after costs? Premium income can help when the stock is flat or rises moderately. Assigned shares still carry downside risk, and covered calls give up gains above their strikes.

Why these parameters?

A 45-delta put is closer to the stock price than a 30-delta put, generally offering more premium and a greater chance of acquiring shares. A 15-delta call is farther out of the money, leaving more upside after assignment. The roughly three-week maturity keeps the policy short and repeatable. Delta is a strike-selection measure, not an exact assignment probability.

The 90% cash budget retains a buffer; whole-contract rounding and waiting for eligible trades leave additional cash unused. Requiring calls to meet the gross purchase price avoids agreeing to a lower sale price, but does not prevent a market loss or guarantee that a suitable call is available.

These parameters were selected in an earlier eight-setting AMZN study using 2023 returns, then applied unchanged to PG. They were not separately optimized on PG. PG itself was selected after comparing four stocks, so this remains an exploratory case study.

Execution rules, data and verification
  1. Contract selection. Use the prior session’s stock close, delta and activity. Monthly PG contracts must have 14–28 calendar days to expiry and absolute delta within 0.10 of the target. Rank by delta distance, maturity distance, strike and RIC. Puts are below the stock price; calls are above it and at least the shares’ gross purchase price. The fixed strike grid is $60–$250 in $5 increments, not the full historical chain.
  2. Liquidity and fills. Require prior-session volume ≥ 100, open interest ≥ 500, positive two-sided quotes, and a spread no greater than $1 and 25% of midpoint. Execute the selected contract at the next session’s bid only if that session’s quote passes the same price filters. Otherwise skip without substituting a different contract. Buybacks use observed ask. Stock sales use bid. No midpoint fills are assumed.
  3. Funding and costs. Put lots = floor(90% × available cash ÷ (100 × strike)), before receiving premium. Calls cover the owned shares, with one short option series at a time. Each option costs $0.65 per contract per side. Stock and assignment fees are $0; idle cash earns 0%; taxes and regulatory fees are omitted.
  4. Expiration and exit. Hold to expiry without discretionary rolls. An option at least $0.01 in the money at the stock close is assumed physically assigned; otherwise it expires. Record 100 shares per contract at the strike, with all lots in a batch using the same convention. Remaining stock and options close at observed sides on the final date.
  5. Dividends and benchmark. Ownership before ex-date trading earns a gross dividend receivable. It contributes to equity immediately and becomes spendable cash on the pay date or the next observed session. The benchmark invests $50,000 at the initial stock ask, permits fractional shares, and reinvests paid dividends at ask. The cash-dividend alternative is also retained. Legal settlement balances are not reconstructed.
  6. Valuation and early exercise. Mark stock at bid and short options at ask. Missing asks can be carried for at most two sessions for valuation, never fills; longer gaps and missing terminal prices fail the run. A separate scenario assigns an ITM covered call just before an announced ex-date when bid-side time value is below the dividend. It returned 22.51% for PG with 0 triggered pre-dividend assignments. This does not establish that actual early assignment was impossible.

Evidence for the fill assumptions

Across 21 opening sales and 63 contracts, the median observed bid–ask width was $0.06 per share; the 90th percentile was $0.15. Selling at bid rather than midpoint reduced receipts by $270.00 before commissions. These spreads support an observable execution-cost assumption. Daily quotes do not establish simultaneous NBBOs, depth, or guaranteed fills.

Research selection and limitations

PG was selected after comparing AMZN, KO, PG and VZ using the same wheel rules. The common study window was fixed before those comparison returns were calculated. It ends September 30, 2024 because LSEG’s daily request cap left later option coverage incomplete; the last common complete quarter supported identical dates for every stock. The four-stock record and earlier AMZN research remain available.
