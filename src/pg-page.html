Three trading rules

1 / CASH-SECURED PUT

Sell a 45-delta put

Choose a strike below PG’s stock price, targeting 21 calendar days to expiry. Reserve enough cash to buy every share if assigned.

2 / COVERED CALL

Sell a 15-delta call

After assignment, sell calls against the owned shares. Require a strike above the stock price and at least its gross purchase price.

3 / REPEAT THE CYCLE

Keep the account funded

Put obligations use up to 90% of available cash, in whole 100-share contracts. Hold to expiry; after call delivery, return to puts.

Research question

Can option premiums improve the return from owning PG after costs? Premium income can help when the stock is flat or rises moderately. Assigned shares still carry downside risk, and covered calls give up gains above their strikes.

Why these parameters?

A 45-delta put is closer to the stock price than a 30-delta put, generally offering more premium and a greater chance of acquiring shares. A 15-delta call is farther out of the money, leaving more upside after assignment. The roughly three-week maturity keeps the policy short and repeatable. Delta is a strike-selection measure, not an exact assignment probability.

The 90% cash budget retains a buffer; whole-contract rounding and waiting for eligible trades leave additional cash unused. Requiring calls to meet the gross purchase price avoids agreeing to a lower sale price, but does not prevent a market loss or guarantee that a suitable call is available.

These parameters were selected in an earlier eight-setting AMZN study using 2023 returns, then applied unchanged to PG. They were not separately optimized on PG. PG itself was selected after comparing four stocks, so this remains an exploratory case study.
